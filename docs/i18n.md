# French translation contract

A language setting switches everything a person reads to French: interface, 3D labels, parts, torques, jobs, safety warnings, faults, fault codes, plans, print-outs and share text. English stays the default. The source language is English, and the English string is the key.

## Shape

- `src/i18n.js`: `t(english, vars)` for interface strings, with `{name}` placeholders, plus plural and number helpers. It falls back to English.
- `i18n/source/*.json`: extracted English strings, split into groups. Generated by `tools/i18n-extract.mjs`.
- `i18n/fr/*.json`: `{english: french}` for each group, owned by translators.
- `dist/fr.js`: built from `i18n/fr/`, loaded by script tag only when French is chosen, so the main page stays under 1 MB and it still works from `file://`. The service worker caches it.
- Data text swaps in place when the language changes, driven by one list of translatable fields in `src/data.js`. Search reindexes in the active language and also matches English.

Never translate: ids, part numbers, fault codes, source titles and URLs, vendor names, brand names (Jaguar, Nikasil, ZF, Brembo, Bilstein and the like), engine codes, gearbox names, tyre sizes, variant labels such as XK8 4.0.

## Translation rules

1. Meaning is exact. Add nothing, drop nothing, and do not improve the English. Keep every hedge such as "about", "may", "one owner reports" and "not sourced".
2. **Every number, unit, range, code and part number stays exact.** `tools/i18n-check.mjs` compares digits between English and French. A torque that changes in translation can break a car or hurt someone.
3. Keep digits as digits, and keep placeholders `{like_this}` unchanged.
4. Decimals use a comma and thousands a thin space: 3.57 becomes 3,57 and 60,000 becomes 60 000. Ranges stay as written: 88-102 Nm. Units stay: Nm, lb ft, mm, mph, miles, litres becomes litres or L as in the source.
5. Speak to the reader as *vous*. Instructions use the imperative: *Déposez l'étrier.* Headings and labels use short noun forms: *Dépose de l'étrier*.
6. Warnings stay as strong as the English. *Do not* becomes *Ne ... pas*, never softer.
7. Warning-light and dashboard messages stay in English capitals as they appear on the car, with the French meaning in brackets: RESTRICTED PERFORMANCE (performances limitées).
8. UK-only things get a natural French equivalent, keeping the original where needed: MOT becomes contrôle technique (MOT). Prices stay in pounds.
9. Plain French a home mechanic would use, with correct workshop vocabulary. No anglicisms where a real French term exists.

## How it is wired

For translators:

- `i18n/source/<group>.json` is an array of English strings. Write `i18n/fr/<group>.json` as `{english: french}`, one entry per string, with the English copied exactly. Groups: ui, jobs, parts, dtc, reference, advice, tutorials, terms.
- A key may end in `|note`, as in `Stop|engine` and `Stop|urgency`. The note says which sense is meant. Leave it out of the French. `|one` marks the singular of a count: the English is the same but the French is singular.
- Keep a trailing space or colon as it is: `Interval: ` becomes `Intervalle : `. The check fails if the space at either end changes.
- A `ui` string of the form `word: word, word` is a search synonym. The first word is what a person types, the rest is what it finds. Use single words and keep the colon and commas.
- Prices: `£120` becomes `120 £`.
- `node tools/i18n-check.mjs <group> --partial` checks a group as it grows. A group named on the command line must have a file, even with `--partial`.

For code:

- The `find` lines in `PAGES` and `BASICS` (`src/data.js`) are single search words, not sentences. Translate them as words a French owner would type.
- The symptom table `SAYS` in `src/ui/diagnose.js` is matched on folded text in both languages. A chip's French label has to match its own row.
- `src/i18n.js` has `t(english, vars)`, `N(english)` to mark a table entry for extraction, `plural(n, one, other, vars)`, and `num dec fixed pct pounds fmtDate collate fold`. `rich()` in `widgets.js` puts elements inside a translated sentence.
- `terms.json` has `term` and `def` in the group `terms`. Its `wordsFr` are forms to find in French text, kept in the data, so a tap-to-explain word has to match the French the translators used. `tools/validate-data.mjs` checks one word belongs to one term.
- Put a car and its year through `carYear(v)`, whose string `{my} {car}` is `{car} de {my}` in French.
- Film titles and channel names stay as published, so `tools/i18n-sweep.mjs` skips them, on the card and in the play button's label.
- `TRANSLATABLE` in `src/data.js` lists the text fields. `translateData()` swaps them in place. `en(rec, field)` gives the English: any code that reads meaning out of data text must use it.
- `app.setLang(lang)` loads French, swaps the data and sets `store.lang`. Everything repaints from `lang`.
- After changing strings, run `node tools/i18n-extract.mjs`. It fails on English outside `t()`. `node tools/i18n-sweep.mjs --pseudo --layout` marks every source string and reports what still shows unmarked, or no longer fits.
